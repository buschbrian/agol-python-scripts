Millcreek 2 m contours (2023 lidar), planning estimate

Ground contours every 2 m (index lines every 10 m) for Millcreek, Utah, from the 0.5 m bare-earth terrain of the 2023 lidar. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

Lines of equal ground elevation across Millcreek, Utah, drawn from the 0.5 m bare-earth terrain of the 2023 lidar.

  • Interval: 2 metres (about 6.56 ft). Index contours every 10 metres (about 32.8 ft). The contours are in metres above NAVD88, not feet.
  • 15,149 lines (3,260 of them index contours), 2,447 km in all, from 1,290 m to 2,080 m (about 4,232 ft to 6,824 ft).

Legend and scales

Lines Shown from Meaning
Index contours 1:288,895 (zoom level 10) inward Every 10 m of elevation (1,290, 1,300, 1,310 m ...)
2 m contours 1:18,056 (zoom level 14) inward Every 2 m between the index lines

This is a vector tile layer: it draws only. The tiles carry no attributes, so the lines have no elevation labels, no pop-ups and no identify. To read an elevation, count from a labelled index line or use the Millcreek bare-earth terrain layer. At small scales the tiling simplifies the lines to the pixel size.

How it was made

Contours at a 2 m interval were traced from the 0.5 m bare-earth terrain (interpolated from the lidar's ground returns), for the city only; every fifth line (each multiple of 10 m) is marked as an index contour. They were packaged as vector tiles for levels 10 to 17; clients draw deeper by overzooming.

Accuracy and checks

  • The hosted lines fall on 2 m multiples of the source terrain with no shift (the best-fitting shift on a 7 cm search grid is zero), and tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's.
  • Tile vertices sit a median 6 cm from the source lines (half a tile unit), against 0.9 m if the datum transformation had been left out.
  • The terrain has not been checked against field survey. QL1 lidar is specified to 10 cm vertically on open ground; errors are larger under dense vegetation and buildings, where the ground is interpolated.

Limits

  • At 0.5 m the 2 m lines follow every curb, berm and driveway cut. They show the ground as it was in late 2023.
  • Under buildings the ground is interpolated, so lines crossing a building are not observed ground.
  • Not for engineering design, grading plans or flood elevations; those need a survey.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. For these products the building and vegetation classes of the points were refined; the ground class was not changed. All products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.

Questions or corrections: City of Millcreek GIS. The processing records (scripts, checks and run logs) are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.
