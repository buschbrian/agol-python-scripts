Millcreek tree crowns (2023 lidar), planning estimate

400,063 estimated tree crown areas around lidar treetops for Millcreek, Utah, by treetop height class. Estimated canopy regions, not surveyed crowns. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

The canopy of Millcreek, Utah, divided into estimated crowns: for each lidar treetop, the patch of canopy at least 2 m tall that belongs to it. 400,063 crowns of at least 3 m2 cover 826.4 ha (median crown 12.25 m2, about 4 m across). They show the shape and spread of the urban forest and which canopy belongs to which treetop. They are estimated canopy regions, not surveyed crowns: a broad tree can be split into several crowns and a clump of small trees can form one.

Map Viewer's legend does not list vector tile layers, so this table is the key to the colours. The layer draws down to about 1:2,257 (zoom level 17); ArcGIS web maps and apps stop drawing it at larger scales. The class names come from the layer's style (one style layer per class). This is a vector tile layer: it draws only. The tiles carry no attributes, so there are no pop-ups, no identify and no filtering; each crown's area, diameter and height are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.

How it was made

  1. Seeds. Every treetop of the Millcreek treetops layer (527,140), plus peaks just outside the city, which only stop city crowns from spreading past the boundary.
  2. Growing crowns. A priority flood over the canopy at least 2 m tall (smoothed by one cell): each canopy cell goes to the treetop it reaches along the path that stays highest, so crowns divide along the low saddles between trees.
  3. Tiles. The city was processed in 63 tiles of 950 m, each with a 40 m overlap; each cell was decided once. Crowns smaller than 3 m2 were dropped: 127,077 treetops have no crown in this layer for that reason.

Accuracy and checks

  • Tiling. On three test tiles, including the densest canopy in the city, crowns grown in tiles with a 20 m overlap were identical, cell for cell, to crowns grown in one piece; the city run used twice that overlap, and rerunning six tiles with an 80 m overlap changed no cell.
  • Tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's; crown vertices sit a median 6 cm from the source polygons (0.7 m if the datum transformation had been left out).
  • No crown has been compared with a field-measured crown or a tree inventory.

Limits

  • Crowns depend on the treetops: a missed treetop's canopy goes to a neighbour, and a false treetop (see the treetops layer's SUSPECT flags, 6.3%) takes canopy from one.
  • Canopy that no treetop reached (8.6 ha, mostly beyond the city) is not drawn. Crowns near the city limits can extend up to 20 m past them.
  • Crown area counts canopy at least 2 m tall seen from above; overlapping crowns of different trees are divided, not shared.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. For these products the building and vegetation classes of the points were refined; the ground class was not changed. All products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.

Questions or corrections: City of Millcreek GIS. The processing records (scripts, checks and run logs) are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.
