Millcreek hillshade 0.5 m (2023 lidar), planning estimate

Shaded relief of the bare ground in Millcreek, Utah, at 0.5 m from the 2023 lidar, for use as a background under other layers. Display only. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

The shape of the bare ground across Millcreek, Utah, lit as if by a sun in the north-west, with buildings and trees removed. It is a detailed background for other layers: streets, channels, terraces, embankments and the east-bench foothills stand out at 0.5 m. It is transparent outside the city limits, so it sits over any basemap.

Legend

Grey levels from dark (ground facing away from the light) to light (ground facing it). The grey values are for display only: they are not elevations or slopes and should not be measured or classified. For elevation use the Millcreek bare-earth terrain layer; for slope use the Millcreek slope classes layer.

Useful from about 1:2,311,162 (zoom level 8) inward; the finest level is 18 (about 0.45 m per pixel on the ground). It works well under other layers with a multiply blend or partial transparency.

How it was made

  1. The 0.5 m bare-earth terrain, interpolated from the lidar's ground returns.
  2. Hillshade with the sun at 315 degrees (north-west) and 45 degrees above the horizon, without cast shadows, computed on the whole lidar mosaic and clipped to the city.
  3. Packaged as 256-pixel PNG tiles with transparency, zoom levels 0 to 18, on the ArcGIS Online tiling scheme. The brightest values (253 and 254, on 0.18% of cells) were lowered to 252 because the tiling tool treats 253 as transparent.

Accuracy and checks

  • At level 18, 2,752 tiles hold the city and all are present; no transparent pixel appears inside the city.
  • Sampled tile pixels match the source hillshade to a median of 1 grey level (95th percentile 3), when read in the right position; read 1.2 m off (no datum transformation) they would differ by a median of 3 and up to 107 levels.
  • Tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's.
  • The terrain under it has not been checked against field survey (QL1 lidar is specified to 10 cm vertically on open ground).

Limits

  • Under buildings the ground is interpolated, so building sites look smoother than they are.
  • At zoom levels 10 to 12 a thin sliver at the city edge is not drawn; at levels 0 to 4 the city is under two pixels across.
  • It shows the ground as it was in October and November 2023.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. The points were reclassified for these products (see "How it was made"); the products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.

Questions or corrections: City of Millcreek GIS. The processing records (scripts, checks and run logs) are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.
