Millcreek slope classes 0.5 m (2023 lidar), planning estimate

Ground slope in six classes (under 10% to 30% and over) for Millcreek, Utah, from the 0.5 m bare-earth terrain of the 2023 lidar. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

The steepness of the bare ground across Millcreek, Utah, in six classes of percent slope, mapped at 0.5 m from the 2023 lidar. It answers questions such as "where is the ground steeper than 30%?" for hillside planning, and shows far more detail than slope layers made from 1 m to 10 m elevation models: street edges, embankments, retaining walls and channels appear as well as the east-bench hillsides.

Percent slope is rise over run times 100: 10% is a 1 m rise in 10 m (about 5.7 degrees); 30% is about 16.7 degrees; 100% is 45 degrees.

The legend labels come from the layer's style (one style layer per class). This is a vector tile layer: it draws only. The tiles carry no attributes, so there are no pop-ups, no identify and no filtering by class. Classes can be switched on and off, or recoloured, in the style.

How it was made

  1. Slope in percent from the 0.5 m bare-earth terrain with ArcGIS Pro's Surface Parameters over a 3 m neighbourhood, computed on the whole lidar mosaic and then clipped to the city, so cells at the city limit use their full neighbourhood.
  2. Each cell put into one of the six classes.
  3. Cleanup (4 m2 minimum mapping unit). Classified as it is, the 0.5 m slope makes 924,458 patches, most under 4 m2, because on a graded hillside slope wanders back and forth across a class break. Every patch smaller than 16 cells (4 m2) was merged into its largest neighbouring patch; nothing else changed. Polygon edges follow the 0.5 m cells; there is no smoothing.
  4. Converted to 129,352 polygons and packaged as vector tiles (levels 0 to 17; clients draw deeper by overzooming).

Accuracy and checks

  • The polygons' areas equal the cleaned class raster's cells exactly for every class, and together they cover the whole city (33,453,372 m2).
  • The cleanup moves the area at or above each threshold by at most 0.54 points of city area, but it shrinks the narrow middle classes (25% to 30% loses 15% of its area to its neighbours) and removes real steep features smaller than 4 m2, such as curb faces and short walls. For exact class areas, use the uncleaned version, available from City of Millcreek GIS.
  • Tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's; the tile vertices sit a median 6 cm from the source polygons (half a tile unit), against 0.7 m if the datum transformation had been left out.
  • The slope itself has not been checked against field measurements. Its accuracy follows the terrain's: QL1 lidar is specified to 10 cm vertically on open ground, and errors are larger under dense vegetation and buildings, where the ground is interpolated.

Limits

  • Slope over 3 m measures the local ground surface, including man-made edges. Expect much more "30% and over" along streets, driveways and embankments than on slope maps from coarser elevation models.
  • Under buildings the terrain is interpolated, so slope there is not meaningful.
  • Not a geotechnical or hazard assessment. Slope alone says nothing about soil, stability or ordinance status.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. The points were reclassified for these products (see "How it was made"); the products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.
