Millcreek 3D buildings LOD1.2 (2023 lidar), planning estimate

21,561 Millcreek, Utah, buildings as flat-roofed 3D blocks (LOD1.2): County footprints raised to their median 2023 lidar roof height. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

Every Salt Lake County building footprint in Millcreek, Utah, that has a lidar roof, as a simple 3D block (CityGML level of detail 1.2): the footprint's outline, from the ground to a flat top at the building's median roof height. 21,561 buildings. They show the city's massing in a web scene: building heights, the scale of streets and how buildings sit on the terrain. Roof shapes are not modelled.

Each block carries its footprint's County attributes and lidar roof-height statistics (see the data dictionary), so a pop-up can show its height. The same statistics, for all 22,042 footprints, are in the Millcreek buildings and canopy cover zones feature layer; the two join on SOURCE_OID.

Using it in a scene

Put the Millcreek bare-earth terrain elevation layer in the scene's Ground, above World Elevation. The blocks were built on that terrain, so they stand on it exactly. On Esri's World Elevation alone they can float or sink by up to about a metre (see Coordinates).

How it was made

  1. Heights. For each County footprint, the median of lidar roof elevation minus bare-earth terrain over its 0.5 m cells (roof where at least two of three independent classifications agree that a point is building). The same figures as HEIGHT_P50_M in the feature layer.
  2. Blocks. ArcGIS Pro's Extract LOD2 Buildings tool at LOD1.2, from a 0.5 m surface that is the lidar roof inside the roofs and the terrain elsewhere, with the County footprints. Each block runs from the ground around the footprint to the median roof. 21,562 footprints with a height went in; one sliver of 0.5 m2 (FP_ID 21952) gave no block.
  3. Packaged as a scene layer package in WGS84 and published.

Accuracy and checks

  • Of 14,229 footprints with lidar roof on at least 95% of their area, the block top is within 1 m of the median roof for all but 13. Of the 2,380 with less than 80% roof (LOW_ROOF_SUPPORT in HEIGHT_STATUS), 695 are more than 1 m off, mostly low. 718 blocks in all are more than 1 m from the median roof (TOP_MINUS_ROOF50).
  • Block bases sit a median 0.11 m below the lowest terrain under the footprint. Four sit 3.6 to 7.2 m above it (FP_ID 22003, 16604, 4881, 6917): fully roofed sites where the tool read the ground near roof level.
  • The hosted layer's own statistics give 21,561 buildings with FP_ID 1 to 22,042, exactly the source's.
  • Roof classification on one hand-labelled 1 km tile (a hard sample, not a citywide accuracy): 92.8% of roof parts found, 2.8% of non-roof parts called roof. No block height has been compared with a survey or plan set.

Limits

  • Heights are roof-minus-terrain estimates, not code-defined building heights. Pitched roofs are shown flat at their median height, so ridges are higher than the block and eaves lower.
  • One block per County footprint. Footprints that hold several buildings, or buildings split across footprints, are shown as the County drew them; buildings missing from the County layer or built since are absent.
  • Low-support footprints (LOW_ROOF_SUPPORT) often have a lower roof part missed by the lidar classification, so their blocks are too low or too high over part of the footprint.

Coordinates

Horizontal: WGS84, taken from NAD83(2011) with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011, the same as every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Vertical: the layer is labelled EGM96 (EPSG 5773), but its heights are NAVD88 (Geoid18) metres, left as they were measured. The packaging tool assigned the label because the source carried no vertical system. In the Salt Lake Valley the two surfaces differ by well under a metre, so on Esri's World Elevation the blocks sit within about that of the ground; on the Millcreek bare-earth terrain they match exactly. Block elevations run from 1,294.1 m to 1,764.2 m.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. The points were reclassified for these products (see "How it was made"); the products are gridded at 0.5 m.

HEIGHT_STATUS flags

Every block carries UNVALIDATED, plus any of: LOW_ROOF_SUPPORT (lidar roof on under 80% of the footprint's cells; a review flag, not an accuracy threshold), NEGATIVE_HEIGHT (at least one cell with roof below terrain, kept), PARTIAL_AOI (the footprint crosses the city limit; statistics use the part inside) and GEOMETRY_REPAIRED (the County polygon was invalid and was repaired). Footprints with no roof support have no block.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.
