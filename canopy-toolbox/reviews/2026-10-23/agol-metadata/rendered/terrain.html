Millcreek bare-earth terrain 0.5 m (2023 lidar), planning estimate

Bare-earth ground elevation for Millcreek, Utah, at 0.5 m from the 2023 lidar, in metres NAVD88, as an elevation layer for 3D scenes. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

The elevation of the bare ground (buildings and vegetation removed) across Millcreek, Utah, and the surrounding lidar coverage, at 0.5 m. It is an elevation layer: use it as the ground of a web scene, where it replaces Esri's coarser World Elevation with the 2023 lidar ground. The Millcreek 3D buildings were built on this terrain and stand on it exactly.

Using it in a scene

In Scene Viewer, add this layer to the scene's Ground, above World Elevation. Keep World Elevation underneath: this layer covers only the lidar area (51.0 km2, about 1.5 times the city), and World Elevation fills in around it. Where the two meet, mostly in the foothills and neighbouring cities, expect a small step along the edge of the lidar tiles.

How it was made

  1. The lidar's ground-classified returns (class 2) were interpolated to a 0.5 m bare-earth surface. Under buildings and dense vegetation the ground is interpolated from the nearest ground returns.
  2. The surface was cached as elevation tiles (LERC, 0.01 m) on the ArcGIS Online elevation tiling scheme, in Web Mercator with the ITRF08 transformation, and published as a hosted elevation layer.

Accuracy and checks

  • Compression and tiling. 300 points sampled from the package (150 flat, 150 steep) against the source terrain at the same position: median difference 0.005 m; maximum 0.010 m on flat ground and 0.012 m on steep ground. Read 1.2 m off (no datum transformation), steep points would differ by up to 2.8 m.
  • Against the source cell a clicked point falls in, differences reach 0.8 m on steep ground (median 0.06 m), from re-gridding to 0.45 m tiles, not from compression.
  • Tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's, and the tile counts per level equal the package's.
  • The ground itself has not been checked against field survey. QL1 lidar is specified to 10 cm vertically (RMSEz) on open, non-vegetated ground; this project's vendor accuracy report has not been reviewed. Errors are larger under dense vegetation and buildings, where the ground is interpolated.

Limits

  • Ground as it was in October and November 2023; grading since then is not reflected.
  • Under buildings the ground is interpolated, so building sites are smoothed.
  • Not for engineering design, grading, flood elevations or surveying.
  • The edge of the coverage follows the 1 km lidar tiles, so it is a staircase.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. The points were reclassified for these products (see "How it was made"); the products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.

Questions or corrections: City of Millcreek GIS. The processing records (scripts, checks and run logs) are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.
