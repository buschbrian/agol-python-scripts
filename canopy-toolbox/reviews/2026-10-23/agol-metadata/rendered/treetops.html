Millcreek treetops (2023 lidar), planning estimate

527,140 treetops at least 2 m tall detected in the 2023 lidar for Millcreek, Utah, by height class. Detections, not a tree census. Unvalidated planning estimate; not surveyed or field-verified.

Planning estimate. This layer is an unvalidated estimate derived from lidar by City of Millcreek GIS. It has not been surveyed, engineered or checked in the field. Use it for planning, screening and display, not for legal, engineering, property-boundary, permitting or code-compliance decisions.

What it shows

The tops of trees across Millcreek, Utah, as found in the 2023 lidar: 527,140 local high points of the canopy at least 2 m above the ground, each drawn as a dot coloured by its height. They give a citywide picture of where trees are and how tall they are. They are detections, not a count of trees: a broad tree can have several tops and a tree under a taller one can be missed.

Dots run from pale yellow-green (2 to 5 m) to dark green (20 m and over) (ColorBrewer YlGn) and grow larger with height. Map Viewer's legend does not list vector tile layers, so this table is the key to the colours. The layer draws down to about 1:2,257 (zoom level 17); ArcGIS web maps and apps stop drawing it at larger scales. The class names come from the layer's style (one style layer per class). This is a vector tile layer: it draws only. The tiles carry no attributes, so there are no pop-ups, no identify and no filtering; a treetop's exact height, ID and parcel ownership are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.

How it was made

  1. Canopy height model. Every lidar point was classified: this project's own classification refined with the IGN FRACTAL model, with buildings decided by a 2-of-3 vote that adds Esri's building model. The canopy height model is the vegetation surface minus the bare-earth terrain at 0.5 m, with building roofs excluded.
  2. Treetops. Local maxima of the canopy height model (smoothed by one cell), searched in a window that grows with height: 1 m radius for canopy 2 to 6 m, 1.5 m for 6 to 12 m, 2 m for 12 to 20 m and 2.5 m above 20 m. Peaks under 2 m and outside the city limits were dropped.

Accuracy and checks

  • On one hand-labelled 1 km tile (a deliberately hard sample, not a citywide accuracy), 95.9% of tree parts were classified as vegetation and 10.7% of non-vegetation parts were wrongly called vegetation.
  • 32,986 treetops (6.3%) are flagged SUSPECT by independent geometric checks (for example a roof edge, a flat top, a wire, a pole or a wall nearby). The flags are review hints, not decisions; they have not been reviewed and the SUSPECT treetops are still drawn here.
  • No treetop has been checked against a field inventory, so the detection rate (trees found) and the false-detection rate are not known.
  • Every package tile was decoded and its points counted: 527,127 of the 527,140 treetops are accounted for; the last 13 are most likely on edges between tiles at different levels. Tiles fetched from the hosted layer are byte for byte the package's, and points sit a median 6 cm from their source positions (1.6 m if the datum transformation had been left out).

Limits

  • Lidar sees the top of the canopy. Small trees under larger ones, and trees under 2 m, are under-counted; broad or multi-stemmed trees can show several tops.
  • Some detections are not trees: structures, poles, wires and roof edges can make peaks (see SUSPECT above).
  • Heights are canopy height above interpolated ground at 0.5 m, not a measured tree height.
  • The lidar was flown in October and November 2023. Trees removed or planted since then are not reflected.

Coordinates and heights. Built in NAD83(2011) / UTM zone 12N (EPSG 6341), metres, and taken to WGS84 / Web Mercator for ArcGIS Online with the transformation WGS_1984_(ITRF08)_To_NAD_1983_2011. Every layer in the Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set uses this same transformation, so the layers line up with each other and with Esri's basemaps to a few centimetres; without it they would sit about 1.2 m apart. Elevations are NAVD88 (Geoid18) in metres; heights are metres above the lidar bare-earth terrain.

Lidar source. The 2023 Salt Lake Valley QL1 lidar (USGS project UT_2023SaltLakeCo_C24, USGS 3D Elevation Program), flown on October 7, November 2 and November 4, 2023. Quality level 1 means at least 8 pulses per square metre and a vertical accuracy (RMSEz) of 10 cm or better on open, non-vegetated ground under the USGS specification; the vendor's own accuracy report for this project has not been reviewed for these products. For these products the building and vegetation classes of the points were refined; the ground class was not changed. All products are gridded at 0.5 m.

The Millcreek LiDAR 2023 set. Eight layers from the same lidar and the same processing: buildings and canopy cover zones (feature layer), 3D buildings (scene layer), bare-earth terrain (elevation layer), hillshade (tile layer), slope classes, 2 m contours, treetops and tree crowns (vector tile layers). They share one coordinate transformation and one terrain, so they can be stacked in one map or scene.

Questions or corrections: City of Millcreek GIS. The processing records (scripts, checks and run logs) are kept by City of Millcreek GIS.
