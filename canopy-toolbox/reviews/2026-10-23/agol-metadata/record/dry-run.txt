    +What it shows
    +Two layers for Millcreek, Utah:
    +Buildings (22,042 polygons):
    +Salt Lake County's building footprints inside or crossing the city limits, each with
    +roof-height statistics measured from the 2023 lidar. 21,562 footprints have a height; 480 have no lidar roof inside
    +them and their height fields are empty (possible reasons include buildings demolished since the County drew them,

    +city and for groups of land by ownership (private, public and its owner groups, uncertain owner, and land outside every
    +parcel).
    +Citywide canopy cover is 25.59% of the observed area (2,103 acres).
    +Typical uses: a citywide look at building heights and massing, finding tall or unusual buildings for review, canopy goals
    +and comparisons between ownership groups.
    +Heights are roof minus ground, not building heights as a building code
    +defines them.
    +How it was made
    +Roofs.
    +Every lidar point was classified three independent ways: this project's own first classification, the
    +IGN FRACTAL model, and Esri's pretrained building model.
    +A point counts as roof when at least two of the three call it
    +building.
    +The roof surface is the highest first or single return in each 0.5 m cell from those points.
    +Ground.
    +The bare-earth terrain is interpolated at 0.5 m from the ground-classified returns (under a building it is
    +interpolated from the ground around it).
    +Heights.
    +For every 0.5 m cell whose centre lies inside a footprint (and inside the city), roof elevation minus
    +terrain elevation.
    +A footprint's median (HEIGHT_P50_M), 95th percentile and maximum are taken over the cells that have
    +both a roof and terrain value.
    +Footprint shapes and County attributes are unchanged.
    +Canopy.
    +A canopy height model at 0.5 m: vegetation returns minus terrain, with roofs masked out.
    +Canopy is any
    +observed cell at least 2 m above the ground.
    +Cover for a zone is canopy cells as a share of the zone's observed cells,
    +counted by cell centres.
    +Zones.
    +From the City's parcel layer: parcels grouped by owner with fixed rules (named public agencies, schools,
    +housing authorities and districts are public; everyone else, including utilities, churches and nonprofits, is private;
    +missing or mixed owners are uncertain), dissolved by group and clipped to the city. "No parcel" is the city outside every
    +parcel, mostly streets and other rights-of-way.
    +Zones overlap: the whole-city zone contains all the others and the
    +public owner groups sit inside "Public / government parcels", so do not add areas across rows.
    +Accuracy and checks
    +Roof classification, against 1,217 hand-labelled roof and non-roof parts on one 1 km tile (a deliberately hard
    +sample, not a citywide accuracy): 92.8% of roofs found; 2.8% of non-roof parts called roof.
    +Vegetation classification, same tile: 95.9% of tree parts called vegetation; 10.7% of non-vegetation parts called
    +vegetation; shrubs 76.5%.
    +Roof support. 2,380 footprints have a lidar roof on less than 80% of their area (LOW_ROOF_SUPPORT).
    +Their heights
    +are less reliable: lower roofs, porches and carports are often the part missed.
    +Of the footprints with at least 95%
    +support, the 3D blocks built from these heights are within 1 m of the median roof for all but 13 of 14,229.
    +Canopy coverage.
    +About 99.4% of every zone is observed by the lidar; the low and high bounds in the layer show how far
    +the unobserved cells could move each figure (about half a point at most).
    +Copies.
    +Every value and every footprint shape was checked against the source library before upload (0 rows
    +differ), and the projection to Web Mercator was checked vertex by vertex (all within 0.07 mm of Esri's own projection
    +with the ITRF08 transformation).
    +None of these figures is a field validation.
    +No building height has been compared with a survey or a plan set.
    +Limits
    +Heights are roof-minus-terrain estimates, not code-defined building heights (which are measured to a defined point
    +of the roof from a defined grade).
    +Chimneys, rooftop equipment, parapets and branches over a roof raise the maximum;
    +the median is the safest single figure.
